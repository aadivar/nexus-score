# Commercial Use of Nexus Score Data

Research Nexus Score's derivative data — scores, sub-scores, rankings,
and derived metrics — is licensed under
[CC BY-NC 4.0](LICENSE-DATA). Non-commercial use is free and requires
only attribution.

**Commercial use requires a separate license.** This page explains what
counts as commercial use, what doesn't, and how to get in touch.

## What counts as commercial use

A use is commercial if it is primarily intended for, or directed toward,
commercial advantage or monetary compensation. In practical terms, the
following require a license:

- Incorporating Nexus Scores, rankings, or sub-scores into a paid
  product, service, dashboard, or platform.
- Vendor or service-provider use that resells, repackages, or
  integrates the data on behalf of paying clients.
- Training, fine-tuning, distilling, or evaluating commercial AI,
  machine learning, or large language models on Nexus Score data,
  rankings, or methodology.
- Using Nexus Score data in the inputs, retrieval layers, embeddings,
  or grounding corpora of any commercial AI system — including
  discovery platforms, recommendation systems, summarisation tools,
  and research assistants.
- Producing derivative datasets primarily for commercial purposes.

## Uses that are generally noncommercial

Subject to the purpose-of-use test below, examples generally include:

- NonCommercial academic and nonprofit research, including benchmarking,
  replication studies, and published findings.
- Library, repository, and university use — procurement analyses,
  collection assessment, internal reporting.
- Journalism, advocacy, and policy work — citing scores, discussing
  rankings, quoting findings.
- Public-interest, non-profit, and educational projects.
- Personal and individual use.

NonCommercial status depends on the use, not the organisation. A nonprofit,
university, charity, government body, or individual can make a commercial
use; a for-profit organisation can sometimes make a NonCommercial use.
Revenue-generating services, paid client engagements, commercial
partnerships, fundraising products, and work directed toward commercial
advantage may require a separate licence regardless of tax status.

If you are unsure where your use falls, please ask before proceeding.

## Honest note on enforcement

This reservation is meaningful but not magical. Determined actors may
ignore it. What it does is:

- Place commercial users on explicit, dated notice that consent was
  not given for unlicensed commercial use — relevant in any
  jurisdiction where the question is contested.
- Create a clear public record of the project's terms, which matters
  for any future structural conversation (foundation funding, PBC
  formation, partnership with a larger entity capable of enforcement).
- Align Research Nexus Score with the broader academic community's
  position on consent and attribution for commercial reuse of
  scholarly infrastructure — see, for example, the Barcelona
  Declaration on Open Research Information.

The intent is to keep Nexus Score useful to the community it was built
for, while making sure that organisations turning it into commercial
revenue — including commercial AI revenue — have at least had to ask.
