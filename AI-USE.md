# AI and Machine Learning Use of Nexus Score

Research Nexus Score data is openly licensed under
[CC BY 4.0](LICENSE-DATA) for almost every use — including by libraries,
researchers, publishers, journalists, discovery platforms, and small
commercial integrations that attribute the source.

There is one exception: **commercial AI / ML training and product
integration**. This page explains what is reserved, what is not, and how
to get a license if you need one.

## What is reserved

The following uses are not granted under CC BY 4.0 and require a separate
license from the author:

- Training, fine-tuning, distilling, or evaluating **commercial** AI,
  machine learning, or large language models on Nexus Score data or
  methodology.
- Using Nexus Score data in the inputs, retrieval layers, embeddings, or
  grounding corpora of **commercial** AI products (discovery platforms,
  recommendation systems, summarisation tools, research assistants, etc.).
- Building derivative datasets where the primary purpose is commercial AI
  training or commercial AI development.

This reservation is intended to constitute a valid machine-readable
opt-out under EU Directive 2019/790 (Article 4), the UK's TDM exception,
and equivalent provisions elsewhere.

A machine-readable version of this reservation is published at the
repository root in [`ai.txt`](ai.txt).

## What is NOT reserved

The CC BY 4.0 license continues to apply, with no AI-specific
restrictions, for:

- **Academic and non-profit AI research.** Including methods research,
  benchmarking, replication studies, and published findings — provided
  the outputs are openly available on terms no more restrictive than
  CC BY 4.0, and the work is not for a commercial product.
- **Internal, non-AI uses of the data.** Libraries running procurement
  analyses, publishers checking their own metadata, journalists writing
  about the field, etc.
- **Non-AI integrations.** Displaying Nexus Scores in a library catalogue,
  publisher dashboard, or institutional report. Using the scores as a
  filter or signal in a non-AI ranking system is also fine.
- **Citing and quoting findings.** Discussing the data, the methodology,
  and specific scores in articles, blog posts, talks, and policy work.

If your use is research, journalism, library work, or any of the above —
you do not need to contact anyone. Just attribute and proceed.

## Honest note on enforcement

This reservation is meaningful but not magical. Determined actors may
ignore it. What it does is:

- Place AI developers on explicit, dated notice that consent was not
  given — relevant in any jurisdiction where unconsented training is
  contested.
- Create a clear public record of the project's terms, which matters
  for any future structural conversation (foundation funding, PBC
  formation, partnership with a larger entity capable of enforcement).
- Align Research Nexus Score with the broader academic community's
  position on consent and attribution for AI training on scholarly
  materials — see, for example, the Barcelona Declaration on Open
  Research Information.

The intent is to keep Nexus Score useful to the community it was built
for, while making sure that organisations turning it into commercial AI
revenue have at least had to ask.
